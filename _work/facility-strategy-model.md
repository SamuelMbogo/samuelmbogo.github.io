A facility that lends concessional capital has to plan its own finances as carefully as it assesses its investees': how much it can deploy, how fast money comes back, and what structure will attract its next funders.

The facility was preparing its strategic plan for 2027 to 2031, backed by a financial model and several draft strategy documents.

My role was to make the numbers hold and to help shape the structure.

I reviewed four draft strategy documents and the financial projections for inconsistencies, then rebuilt the financial model to the FAST modelling standard, which surfaced cash-flow constraints the earlier model had obscured. I also translated a development finance institution's fund-level due diligence feedback into structural options for the facility's next phase, comparing a general partner and limited partner fund structure with a layered facility or separately managed account. The output was presented to the facility's anchor funder.

## What I took from it

The structure of a fund decides who can invest in it. Getting the model right first is what makes that conversation credible.
