When a silent partner leaves a group of companies, the price has to be fair to both sides, even though the partner never ran the business and the group was never built to be valued as one.

The client was a group of ten subsidiaries across three businesses: hospitals and outpatient care, pharmaceutical manufacturing, and clinical services. A silent partner was divesting their stake.

My role was to consolidate the group and help build an independent valuation both parties could negotiate from.

We analysed each subsidiary on its own, removed intercompany transactions and related-party flows so nothing was counted twice, and valued each business with the method that fitted it: DCF for the operating healthcare businesses, net assets for asset-heavy entities, and multiples as a cross-check. The partner's proportionate interest was then assessed for discounts for lack of control and marketability, which compound rather than add.

## What I took from it

In a group, value hides in the connections between companies. Clean the related-party flows first, and the valuation gets much easier to defend.
