When a secured lender extends fresh working capital to a manufacturer that has just come through a USD 40 million default, the question is no longer whether the business survived. It is whether the new money will be used well.

The client, one of Kenya's longest-established industrial manufacturers, had exited a lender-initiated administration after its banks accepted significant write-offs and its shareholders reinvested. It was settling obligations to banks, creditors and the tax authority while running its plants, and it still needed working capital from one of its secured lenders.

My role was to give that lender an independent view it could rely on.

The lender appointed our team as overseers. I compiled the company's monthly management accounts with month-on-month tracking, contributed to its Statement of Affairs, and recommended the liquidity and coverage ratios the business needed to hold to meet its monthly obligations. Every quarter we presented and defended a report to the lender on how its funding was being used, while advising management on expense treatment and operating efficiency.

## What I took from it

Oversight after a restructuring tests judgement under scrutiny. The numbers have to satisfy a credit committee that has already taken one loss, and the advice has to improve the business without loosening the discipline the lender is relying on.
