A small listed agricultural company can look simple on paper: one crop, one estate, one buyer. The risks sit in exactly that simplicity.

The client was an agricultural company listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange, operating through a managing agent that also sat within its controlling shareholder's group.

My role was on the team assessing its risks and how they should shape a view of its value.

We looked at production risk from the concentration of its assets, climate and labour costs; market risk from commodity auction prices and currency movements; and governance risk from a managing-agent arrangement in which related-party pricing and the value of the company's land could favour the controlling shareholder over minorities. For a listed company in a change of control, those risks affect every shareholder reading the annual report.

## What I took from it

Governance is a valuation input. Where a related party sets the price of what a company sells, the minority shareholder's value depends on terms they cannot see.
