No single method values every business well. Asset-heavy agricultural and manufacturing companies often need a net asset view; growing businesses need their cash flows projected; and in East Africa, the comparable companies that make a market check easy are scarce.

Across a series of mandates in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania, I valued businesses in agriculture, education, manufacturing and food, for purposes ranging from transactions to regulatory and strategic needs.

My role was to choose the method that fitted each business and to defend the result.

I applied DCF to businesses whose earnings could be projected, NAV where value sat mainly in assets, and comparable multiples adjusted for East African conditions as a cross-check. Cross-border mandates needed country-specific discount rates, multi-currency cash flows and different tax regimes. The period itself was a lesson in dating inputs: the Kenyan shilling swung sharply between late 2023 and mid-2024, and a discount rate set in one quarter could legitimately differ from one set a few months later.

## What I took from it

A valuation is an argument, and every input is a claim that has to hold up on the date it was made.
