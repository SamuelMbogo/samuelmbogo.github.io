Sub-Saharan Africa manufactures only a small share of the medicines it consumes, so global supply shocks become local shortages. Closing that gap takes industrial infrastructure, and industrial infrastructure takes lenders who believe the numbers.

The client proposed an integrated industrial park in Kenya co-locating pharmaceutical manufacturing, diagnostics, contract manufacturing and shared logistics: twelve distinct asset classes in one USD 150 million development.

My job was to build the one model that commercial banks, development finance institutions and equity partners could all underwrite at the same time.

I architected a ten-year model in which each asset class carried its own revenue drivers, cost structure and phasing before rolling up into a single cash flow available for debt service. Debt was sized against minimum coverage of 1.30x DSCR and 1.40x LLCR within a 70/30 debt-to-equity structure, drawn over a three-year construction period against certified construction milestones, consistent with lender norms for comparable East African infrastructure. The waterfall followed standard project finance priority, with a six-month debt service reserve and distribution locks that held back equity payouts until coverage tests cleared.

The regulatory path mattered as much as the financing. Every manufacturer on the site needs good manufacturing practice certification from Kenya's medicines regulator, so licensing cost and timing were built into the phasing. A risk workshop with the client's team set the stress cases: cost overrun, revenue delay, approval delay, currency and pricing. For development finance readers, the model mapped outcomes against the IFC Performance Standards and SDGs 3, 8 and 9.

The model anchored capital-raising conversations with banks, development finance institutions and equity partners, and the project advanced to the next stage of due diligence.

## What I took from it

A lender reads a model for what can go wrong. Building it that way from the start, reserves, locks and stress cases included, is what turns an ambitious project into a financeable one.
