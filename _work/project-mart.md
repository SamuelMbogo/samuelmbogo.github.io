When a large retailer collapses, the balance sheet it last published is rarely the one creditors can recover from. The work is finding out where the value went, and how much of it can still be reached.

A regional retail chain had failed with liabilities of around KES 20 billion and was in liquidation.

My role was on the forensic team working out what was genuinely recoverable.

I built a dynamic balance sheet tracking the erosion of the company's assets from its pre-collapse position through the liquidation, working from historical financial statements, court filings, bank records and management inputs. The work separated assets that were genuinely recoverable from book values that would not survive scrutiny, and included interviews with individuals connected to the collapse. The investigation verified about KES 2 billion as recoverable for creditors.

## What I took from it

Book value is what the accounts claim. Recoverable value is what survives evidence, legal challenge and the cost of getting it back.
