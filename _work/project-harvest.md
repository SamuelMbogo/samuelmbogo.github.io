Smallholder farmers in displacement-affected parts of East Africa often earn far less than their neighbours, held back by poor access to quality inputs, training that ignores tiny plots, and unreliable buyers. An agribusiness serving these communities had built a model that addressed all three: affordable inputs, practical training and a guaranteed buy-back channel.

A global impact investor was preparing to back it through an impact-linked convertible note, with repayment terms improving as the company reached further impact milestones.

My role was on the team testing whether the investment stood up.

I worked on the company's revenue model and unit economics across input supply and produce buy-back, reviewed working-capital cycles driven by planting and harvest seasons, and carried out a three-year look-back of its financial history. I also assessed the mechanics of the note, including how the impact-linked features (farmers reached, acres under cultivation, people fed) would adjust its terms.

The risks we identified, including seasonal working-capital strain, concentration in certain districts and the challenge of scaling into new areas, were judged manageable. The investment closed.

## What I took from it

Impact due diligence adds one question to a standard review: are the metrics that move the money real, measured consistently and hard to game?
