Development-stage equity is hard to sell. Before a project signs its power purchase agreement, its value rests on the probability that it will; after signing, the stake can be locked in until the plant is operating, which can take years.

The facility held a legacy minority equity stake in a small hydropower project that was approaching its power purchase agreement. Updated legal advice indicated that signing would trigger a lock-in on shareholder exits.

My role was to lead the exit valuation and to work through how the exit could actually happen.

I built a phased valuation of the stake and refined it into three versions for three different readers: the Investment Committee, which needed full assumptions and the downside; prospective buyers, who needed a clear case for value; and management, who needed a summary to decide on. With external counsel, I worked through the exit route: a transfer to a third party against a buy-back by the company, the stamp duty and capital gains tax consequences of each, and the sequencing needed to complete before the lock-in took effect. I prepared the Investment Committee presentation for the exit.

The regulatory backdrop moved during the work. Kenya lifted its moratorium on new power purchase agreements in November 2025, with new vetting and disclosure conditions, which changed both the timetable and what a buyer would need to know.

## What I took from it

An exit is designed, not found. The valuation matters, but the route, the tax and the timing decide whether the value can be realised at all.
