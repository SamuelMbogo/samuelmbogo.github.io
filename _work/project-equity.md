Share options are how many growing companies keep senior people without paying cash they do not have. Accounting for them properly means putting a fair value on something that may never be exercised.

Two Kenyan companies, one private and one listed, used share-based compensation to retain senior talent and needed their plans valued.

My role was to build and review the valuation models.

I applied option-pricing methods, calibrated to each company's share price, exercise terms, volatility, risk-free rate and vesting conditions, and made sure the models met the requirements of IFRS 2 for financial statement disclosure. For the private company, volatility had to be estimated from comparable listed companies. For the listed company, observed market data was available, but thin trading meant it had to be tested rather than simply accepted.

## What I took from it

An observable market price is only as good as the market behind it. The hardest input in an option model is usually the one that looks easiest.
