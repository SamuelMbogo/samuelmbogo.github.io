Most water projects in Kenya do not fail for lack of construction finance. They fail earlier, because nobody pays for the feasibility studies, legal structuring and transaction work that turn an idea into something a lender can underwrite.

KIFFWA exists for that gap. It lends recoverable, below-market development capital to early-stage water and sanitation projects, capped at half of a project's development budget, and recycles the money when projects repay or exit.

My job is to manage the front door: the investment pipeline.

Every proposal enters through a Quick Scan, a structured first screen against the facility's mandate, the project's bankability and the fit of the instrument. Many proposals need a revision round before they are ready, some are held while questions are answered, and a large share are screened out. I track every initiative from first contact to decision, across community schemes, county utilities and national projects, and work with sponsors on what their proposals need to become investable.

I also supported the setup of a public-private partnership framework with the national government ministry responsible for water. Through it, fourteen national bulk-water, dam and desalination projects entered the pipeline for project-preparation support. One county bulk-water scheme that had received early development funding from the facility has since returned to the pipeline at national PPP scale, which is project preparation working the way it is meant to.

## What I took from it

Bankability is made or lost at preparation stage, long before a lender sees a model. Screening well also means saying no clearly, and early, so that a sponsor's time and the facility's capital go where they can do the most.
