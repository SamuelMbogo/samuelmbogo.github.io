Development-stage investments are the least predictable part of infrastructure finance. Studies overrun, sponsors change plans and projects stall, so the work after disbursement matters as much as the decision to lend.

The facility's active portfolio holds eight development-stage investments, with about EUR 2 million committed against about EUR 76 million of underlying project value.

My role is to help keep that portfolio honest.

I contribute to portfolio monitoring and Investment Committee preparation across the investments, track covenant compliance, and collect impact data from investees. I also prepare programme and monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL) reporting to the facility's anchor funder, against the results framework of the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs, tracking outcomes such as people reached with water and sanitation, jobs created and reductions in non-revenue water.

## What I took from it

In concessional finance, reporting is not paperwork. It is how a funder knows whether patient capital is doing what it was lent to do, and whether it will come back to be lent again.
