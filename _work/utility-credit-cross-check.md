A public water utility's investment case usually rests on its own projections. Kenya's water regulator publishes something more useful: standardised performance data for every regulated utility, year after year, prepared for nobody's deal.

A utility had applied to the facility with a strong base case. Its projected returns were high and its assumptions on metering and revenue collection were ambitious.

My role was to test that case against the regulator's record before the facility committed.

I compared the utility's projections with three consecutive years of the regulator's reports. The regulator's figures showed operating cost coverage falling steadily over the period, a downgrade in the utility's creditworthiness index, and actual metering and collection rates well below what the base case assumed. Electricity was also an unusually large share of operating costs, which left the utility exposed to power tariffs.

I recommended keeping the investment on hold and added the findings to the clarification items the sponsor had to answer. Once those items were resolved, the project advanced to a draft letter of no objection and into Investment Committee due diligence.

## What I took from it

Regulator data is the closest thing to an audited history that many public utilities have, and it is freely available. Used early, it makes an investment better rather than simply stopping it.
