A listing puts a company's history in front of the market. For investors to trust it, the numbers have to be consistent, complete and honest about the risks.

A Kenyan manufacturer was preparing to list its shares on the Nairobi Securities Exchange, and needed the accountant's report and financial due diligence that the regulator requires.

My role was on the team preparing both.

We restated several years of historical financial statements onto a consistent IFRS basis, identified adjustments for accounting policy changes and reclassifications, prepared the required per-share and ratio disclosures, and assessed working-capital adequacy with a twelve-month cash flow projection. We also identified the issues that needed disclosing to investors and the regulator, and understood the group structure around the listing entity so that the history presented to the market was clean and consistent.

The listing completed in 2025.

## What I took from it

Disclosure is a form of risk management. The risks a listing document states plainly are the ones investors can price; the ones it leaves out become surprises.
