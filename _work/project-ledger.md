In an acquisition, the most valuable output of due diligence is sometimes a reason not to buy.

A Kenyan commercial bank was considering buying a deposit-taking financial institution to reach small-business and low-income customers. In lending businesses the loan book is the business, so the question was whether the institution's earnings and assets were as strong as its accounts suggested.

My role was to help answer that question on the buyer's side.

We analysed the loan portfolio by product, sector and geography, tested asset quality and provisioning against the central bank's prudential rules, and assessed earnings quality, capital adequacy and regulatory compliance. The adjustments from that work fed directly into whether the proposed price reflected the institution's true position.

The findings on revenue quality did not support the acquisition, and the buyer did not proceed. The target's subsequent public results have borne that judgement out.

## What I took from it

The loss a buyer avoids is as real as the return a buyer earns. It just never shows up in a portfolio report, which is why it takes discipline to value.
