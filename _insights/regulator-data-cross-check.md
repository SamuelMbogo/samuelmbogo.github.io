Every investment application I review arrives with a base-case financial model that looks bankable. That is not a criticism of applicants. It is what a good sponsor does: assumptions get tuned to the story, and by the time the file reaches an investment analyst it has been through internal review, board sign-off and often a consultant's polish. The model works. It always works.

The question a due diligence analyst has to answer is different: does it work at the performance the counterparty has actually delivered? In Kenyan water infrastructure, that answer is publicly available, in a form designed for comparison across utilities. It is the annual Impact Report published by the Water Services Regulatory Board.

## What the Impact Report contains

The report scores every licensed water service provider on the same key performance indicators, including water quality, hours of supply, staff productivity, revenue collection efficiency, non-revenue water, metering, personnel costs as a share of operating costs, and operating cost coverage. It also publishes a creditworthiness index built from operational and financial metrics.

Three consecutive reports read together give a trajectory. That trajectory is often the most powerful single piece of information in an application review, because it exists independently of anything the applicant has said, and in a form the applicant cannot dispute.

## A worked example

In a recent review, a utility's base case assumed metering and revenue collection far above anything in its reported history. Read against three years of the regulator's reports, the picture changed: operating cost coverage had fallen steadily, the creditworthiness index had been downgraded in the latest year, actual metering and collection sat well below the base case, and electricity was an unusually large share of operating costs.

None of this made the project undoable. It made it a different project from the one submitted. A model built on the applicant's metering assumptions produces a materially different cash-flow profile from one built on the utility's actual record. That is not a rounding difference. It is the difference between an investable case and one that will need concessional support at every debt service date.

The recommendation was not to decline. It was to hold the application and send specific questions back to the sponsor, so the base case could be rebuilt on the utility's actual performance and the gap to the projections explained. Once those questions were answered, the project moved forward to formal due diligence.

## Why this discipline matters

Concessional and blended finance exists because commercial capital cannot fully price the risks in projects like these. But the concessional element is not free. It is scarce, it is contested, and every shilling deployed to a project that overpromises is a shilling unavailable to one that would have quietly delivered.

Regulator data is a discipline against the natural wish for deals to work. It does not kill projects. It sharpens them, and it is available to anyone who looks.
